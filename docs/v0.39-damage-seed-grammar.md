# v0.39 shared visual-seed + damage grammar

## Research question

Can one deterministic per-object visual seed replace duplicated facade hashing **and** pay for more expressive destruction states after Roadroller compression?

v0.35 proved that architectural repetition can create more buildings for fewer bytes. v0.38 proved that invisible work can be exchanged for visible street structure. v0.39 asks whether the same principle can make the town feel more reactive while improving renderer structure.

## Structural observation

The generic building renderer derives the same coordinate hash twice:

- `s = o.x + o.y | 0` for lot / roof service marks;
- `g = x + y | 0` later for facade bays, window rows and roof variation.

For buildings, `s` and `g` are identical information expressed through two local variables.

The v0.39 hypothesis is to hoist one deterministic visual seed to the object loop and reuse it for:

```text
lot identity
   +
facade rhythm
   +
rubble silhouette
   +
damage scars
```

No new RNG state is needed. The renderer remains a pure projection of already-existing object state.

## Round one: representation ladder

Same-workflow paired Roadroller results:

| candidate | source Δ | ZIP | packed Δ | rubble states | damage states |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| control | 0 | 13,234 | 0 | 2 | 1 |
| shared-seed | -4 | 13,235 | +1 | 2 | 1 |
| seeded-rubble | -7 | 13,239 | +5 | 16 | 1 |
| **damage-bands** | **-40** | **13,219** | **-15** | **16** | **5** |
| damage-stagger | -20 | 13,246 | +12 | 16 | 5 |

The first clear Pareto winner is `damage-bands`: 40 fewer source bytes, 15 fewer packed bytes, 16 deterministic rubble phases instead of two, and five partial-damage levels instead of one broad overlay.

The other results are equally useful. Hoisting a shared seed is cleaner source but essentially packed-neutral. Seeded rubble alone costs five bytes. Most importantly, injecting seed entropy into every scar (`damage-stagger`) is expensive even though the source is shorter than control.

### Compression lesson

The packer prefers **repeated structural grammar** over local irregularity.

```text
GOOD
same operation × damage level

EXPENSIVE
same operation × damage level × independent seed offset
```

That mirrors the architectural grammar result: variation is cheapest when it comes from a compact repeated language rather than many locally distinct decisions.

## Round two: improve the semantics, not merely the state count

Full-width horizontal bands are extremely cheap, but they risk reading as siding rather than structural damage. Round two keeps the winning low-entropy idea and asks whether we can make the marks look more like fractures without surrendering the packed win.

The active refinement ladder is:

### `damage-bands`
Round-one winner. Five full-width dark scar levels.

### `fracture-alt`
Alternate half-width scars between the left and right halves of the facade.

This adds only a two-phase rhythm and should read more like interrupted floors / broken facade sections than continuous siding.

### `fracture-inset`
The same alternating half-width grammar with a small inset at each end. This tests whether visible breathing room around scars is worth its entropy.

### `bands+wall-dim`
Keep the highly compressible full bands but also darken the facade shell as HP falls. This restores a broad damage cue while retaining discrete structural damage levels.

### `fracture+wall-dim`
Combine alternating half-width fractures with broad wall darkening. This is the strongest semantic candidate if its packed cost remains attractive.

The key constraint is deliberate: **no per-scar randomness**. We want damage to feel irregular through a tiny repeatable rhythm, not through a miniature random-number language.

## Safety boundary

Research branch only until paired evidence exists.

No candidate may change:

- object HP, value or collision geometry;
- damage calculations;
- scoring or takeover progress;
- object placement;
- RNG consumption;
- destruction timing;
- particle emission;
- road or town topology.

Only the visual projection of existing object state may change.

## Promotion gates

A candidate is promotable only if:

1. paired ZIP size is no worse than control, preferably clearly smaller;
2. normal gameplay and packed-runtime tests remain green;
3. destroyed buildings have visibly more varied silhouettes;
4. partial damage remains immediately readable during motion;
5. family identity from v0.35 remains legible under damage;
6. the renderer remains pure and deterministic;
7. exact integrated runtime remains <= 13,312 bytes.

More states are not automatically better art. The preferred result is the smallest repeated visual grammar that communicates **intact → damaged → wrecked** at a glance.
